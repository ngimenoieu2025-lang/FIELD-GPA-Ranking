\subsection{US Course Grades}
\label{sec:data-us}

To see whether the Spanish pattern also appears elsewhere, we used \textit{Course Grades from 33 Public Universities}, released by Jeong et al.\ on Kaggle (``Course Grades''). Each row gives the shares of letter grades awarded in a course or section. It does not describe an individual student's overall GPA. The 33 files contain 1,449,779 raw rows. We removed exact duplicates and invalid grade distributions, and matched approximately 94\% of valid rows to a broad area. We calculated each section's GPA using A+ = 4.3 through F = 0, averaging only the numeric grades and excluding pass/fail outcomes and withdrawals. When combining sections, we gave more weight to those with more enrolled students. Four institutions lack enrolment counts: Georgia Tech, Parkland College, Purdue and UNC Charlotte. This leaves 29 in the weighted analysis. The US records cover 2009--2023, with different years available at each institution, compared with Spain's 2015--2023 window. These differences matter when interpreting the comparison; \S\ref{sec:methods-us} explains how we grouped the subjects and checked the results.
